How we study the consumer mind.

Our research spans four themes, united by a common question: what psychological processes lead people to choose what they choose — and how can that choice environment be understood, predicted, and improved?

Consumer Decision-Making

We study how people evaluate products, prices, and trade-offs under time pressure, uncertainty, and information overload — using choice tasks, eye-tracking, and computational models of preference formation.

Digital & AI-Mediated Consumption

As recommendation systems and generative AI increasingly mediate what we buy, we examine how algorithmic trust, transparency, and personalization shape discovery and purchase behavior.

Emotion, Identity & Brand

We investigate how self-concept, emotion regulation, and social identity motivate brand attachment, status consumption, and the stories people tell through what they buy.

Sustainable & Ethical Consumption

We identify behavioral barriers to pro-social and sustainable choices, and design and test interventions — from framing to default options — that help close the intention-behavior gap.

Our approach

Behavioral experiments

Controlled choice tasks that isolate the cognitive and motivational mechanisms behind everyday consumer decisions.

Computational modeling

Formal models of preference, attention, and learning that let us make quantitative, falsifiable predictions about choice.

Field & platform studies

Partnerships with real platforms and retailers to test how findings generalize outside the lab.

Open, reproducible science

Pre-registration, open materials, and shared data wherever possible, so our findings can be verified and built upon.
